Genius OBD – Privacy Policy

App: Genius OBD (Android, package app.geniusobd).
Effective date: November 24, 2026

Genius OBD ("the app", "we") reads data from your car through an OBD2 adapter (Bluetooth or Wi-Fi) and keeps it on your phone. The app needs no sign-up. This page explains what data the app handles, where it goes, how long it is kept and how to delete it.

Summary

  • No sign-up. The app does not collect your contacts or account data. The app itself never reads your location; Google ads may estimate an approximate location from your internet address (see Ads).
  • Car data stays on your phone. It leaves the phone only for the features you use: send a report for analysis, request norms for your car, or save or share a records archive. When the app opens, it asks our server how many reports you have left.
  • If you have no subscription and no report pack, the app shows ads from Google AdMob: a banner at the bottom of most screens (not the main screen or the purchase screen) and, now and then, a full-screen ad after you finish something. Car data, the VIN, notes and reports are never sent to the ad service.
  • We do not sell your car data, notes or reports. You can opt out of personalized ads in Settings, Ad choices.

Data stored on your phone

  • Trip records: sensor readings, summaries by driving mode, trouble codes, readiness monitors and on-board self-test results.
  • Car details: make, model, year, engine, the VIN and the mileage if you enter it.
  • Your notes recorded in the app, and your answers in the report survey.
  • Received reports and app settings.

This data stays in the app's own storage on your phone. We do not have a copy of it.

Data that leaves the phone

Data leaves the phone only for the features you use:

  • AI report. When you send a report for analysis, a report package goes to our server and from there to Anthropic (USA), the AI provider that writes the analysis. It contains the car make, model and year, engine type, sensor readings and summaries, trouble codes, self-test results, the app's own Car health verdict for each system, dates and times of the trips, your survey answers, note and comments on events, a short summary of the previous report for the same car and the checks from it you marked as done, and the mileage if you entered it. It does not contain the VIN, location, name, phone number or email. Emails, phone numbers and VINs typed into notes and comments are masked before sending; please don't type names, addresses or other personal details there. Some reports are prepared by a person instead: the app's owner may read the package (it has no name and no VIN) to write the report by hand. This can take up to 24 hours.
  • Reports left. When the app opens, it asks our server how many reports you have left. This sends the install ID and, with a subscription, the Google Play purchase token. With a subscription the app also repeats this check in the background about every 10 days, so that renewals are counted.
  • Your reports on the server. When you open AI reports, the app asks our server for the list of reports made for this install (date and overall result) and shows the ones that are not on the phone, for example after reinstalling the app. Downloading them is free.
  • Norms request. If the app has no norms for your car and you tap “Ask to add what's missing” or “Ask to add this car”, the request goes to our server with the install ID, the car make, model, year and engine (or the engine size and fuel you typed in), which systems are missing and the version of the norms database. It does not contain the VIN. We add the norms to the database if we find them in official sources; the app checks for the answer when you open it.
  • Records archive. When you save or share an archive of records, it goes where you choose to send it.

What our server keeps

  • Install ID. The app creates a random install ID. It is shown under Settings, Privacy. The server stores only a salted hash of it, to deliver reports to the right phone and to limit how often reports can be ordered. If Android backup is on, the install ID (and nothing else from the app) is saved in your Google backup, so purchased reports and your reports on the server survive reinstalling the app. If you close the app while a report is being written, the app checks the report status in the background with this ID and shows a notification when it is ready.
  • Report package. Kept only until the analysis is finished (usually a minute or two), then deleted. A package whose report is still being prepared, including by hand, is kept for up to 24 hours; if no report is ready by then, the request is closed and the package is deleted.
  • Reports. The finished report is stored on our server so the app can download it again at any time, and for as long as we need it to check and improve the quality of the analysis. Reports do not contain the VIN, location, name, phone number or email.
  • Request log. Time, size, result code and cost of each analysis and whether it was paid from a subscription or a pack, without the package, for 90 days.
  • Norms requests. The hashed install ID, the car description, the missing systems, the database version and our answer. Deleted 30 days after we answer, or after 90 days if there is no answer. To stop one person from flooding us with requests, we also keep a salted hash of your IP address (not the address itself) for one day, then delete it.
  • Subscription. A salted hash of the Google Play purchase token, the subscription state and end date, and how many reports are left this month. Kept about 13 months after the last check with Google Play.
  • Report packs. If you buy reports without a subscription: a salted hash of the purchase token, the hashed install ID, how many reports are left and when they expire (one year after purchase). Kept about 13 months after they expire. Deleting your data by email does not delete reports you paid for.

Network connection

Everything the app sends to our server travels over an encrypted connection. Our server runs on Cloudflare. When the app connects, Cloudflare receives your IP address, as with any website. Our server uses it only to limit how many requests can come from one address and does not store it (the exceptions are one-day salted hashes for norms requests, described above, and for failed purchase checks, to stop one address from flooding Google Play checks); logging of request addresses and headers is turned off.

Norms database updates

About once a week, and when you tap “Check and update” in Settings, the app downloads a newer version of the norms database from our server if there is one. This request sends nothing about you or your car, only the version the app already has. You can turn off “Check once a week” in Settings.

Purchases and app checks

Subscriptions and report packs are handled by Google Play. The app sends the purchase token to our server, and the server asks Google Play whether the subscription is active or the pack is paid, and checks Google Play for refunds. We do not receive your payment details.

Before an analysis the app also asks Google Play Integrity to confirm that the request comes from the genuine app installed from Google Play; the server checks this answer with Google and does not store it.

Ads

If you have no subscription and no report pack, the app shows two kinds of ads from Google AdMob: a banner at the bottom of every screen except the main screen and the purchase screen, and an occasional full-screen (interstitial) ad, at most once every 10 minutes and never during your first day after installing. A full-screen ad appears only in a pause after you finish something: after you close an AI report, or when you leave Records, Journal, Car health or Emissions test readiness. It is never shown when the app starts or closes, while connecting to the adapter, sending a report, buying, granting permissions, clearing error codes or on the live data screen. No ads are shown while a trip is being recorded. With the adapter connected, ads appear only after it has shown the car standing still for 30 seconds. No ads are shown for 30 seconds after the adapter disconnects, including when you press Stop. Without a connected adapter the app can't tell whether the car is moving. People with a subscription or a report pack do not see ads.

When ads are shown, the Google Mobile Ads SDK collects and shares with Google the advertising ID, app set ID and other device and app identifiers, the IP address (which can indicate approximate location), app interactions such as ad taps, and diagnostic information. Google uses them to show and measure ads, for analytics and to prevent fraud, as described in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

Your choices:

  • On first launch Google may show a message asking for your consent. You can change your choice at any time in the app under Settings, Ad choices.
  • You can reset or delete the advertising ID in Android settings (Settings → Privacy → Ads, or Settings → Google → Ads). Without it you see ads that are not personalized.

We put nothing about your car into ad requests. Car data, the VIN, notes, reports and the install ID are never sent to the ad service.

Data we do not collect

  • The app never reads or stores your location. On Android 11 and older the system requires the location permission to scan for Bluetooth adapters. Google may estimate approximate location from the IP address when it shows ads (see Ads).
  • No contacts and no accounts.

Sharing and sale

We do not sell your car data, the VIN, notes or reports, and we do not share them for advertising. Anthropic processes the report package only to write your report and, under its commercial terms, does not use it to train its models. Google receives what is described under Purchases and app checks and Ads.

Some US state privacy laws treat giving the advertising ID and similar identifiers to an ad network for personalized ads as a “sale” or “sharing” of personal information. You can opt out at any time in Settings, Ad choices, or by deleting the advertising ID in Android settings.

Your privacy rights (US states)

Depending on where you live (for example California, Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia, Texas and other states with consumer privacy laws), you may have the right to:

  • know what personal information we collect and how we use it (this page);
  • get a copy of the personal information our server holds about you;
  • correct or delete it;
  • opt out of the sale or sharing of personal information and of targeted advertising (Settings, Ad choices).

To use these rights, email us with the install ID shown under Settings, Privacy; this is how we find your data, since we do not know your name or email. You can let an authorized agent ask for you. We reply within 45 days. We will not treat you differently, or charge you more, for using these rights. If we decline a request, you can appeal by replying to our answer.

Deleting your data

  • On the phone. "Delete all" under Records in Settings deletes the selected car's trip records, raw logs and event log. Records of other cars are deleted after you switch to each of them in My cars. Car details, the VIN, trip summaries, notes, survey answers and received reports stay until you clear the app's data in Android settings (Settings → Apps → Genius OBD → Storage → Clear data) or uninstall the app.
  • On our server. To delete your reports, norms requests and request log from our server, email namarobd2@gmail.com with the install ID shown in the app under Settings, Privacy. We delete them within 30 days and reply when it is done.
  • At Google. Data collected for ads is handled by Google; see How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

Children

The app is not directed to children under 13 and we do not knowingly collect data from them.
